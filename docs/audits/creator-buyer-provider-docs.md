## Policy classification

| Mode | Provider constraints | Consequence for this audit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduled virtual event, paid Watch-Party, paid video | Apple treats one-to-many real-time services as IAP; physical services consumed outside the app use other methods. Google treats in-app digital content/services as digital purchases. | Selling before Sunday is compatible with a digital entitlement model; timing does not make a virtual show a physical-event ticket. Keep platform billing as instructed. |
| Channel subscription / Premium | Apple subscriptions need ongoing value, explicit benefits and duration. | Keep separately scoped grants; Premium must not silently substitute for creator tickets or channel access. |
| VIP pass | Classification depends on the actual benefit; in-app access remains digital. | Publish precise scope and expiry. A label does not establish event admission or physical seating. |
| Tips | Apple allows IAP tipping. Google exempts pure tips only if the entire contribution reaches the creator and nothing digital is granted. | “100% creator net” is not enough evidence for the Google exemption. Keep existing provider route and split; clarify fees without changing promises. |

## Purchase identity, lifecycle, and recovery

| Boundary | Official evidence | Audit implication / negative control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apple account association | `appAccountToken` associates an app customer with a transaction and is returned in that transaction. [API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreserverapi/appaccounttoken), [purchase option](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/product/purchaseoption/appaccounttoken(_:)) | Bind immutable buyer/source/provider transaction on the backend; a different currently signed-in app user must not claim an old in-flight purchase. |
| Google verification | Verify purchases server-side before benefits; purchase tokens are globally unique; voided-purchase handling revokes access. [Security guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/security) | A reused token must not create another ticket, ledger credit, or grant for another account. Wrong product, package, environment, and source mapping must fail. |
| Pending and recovered purchase | Grant only after `PURCHASED`; pending can finish while the app is closed; query purchases on reconnect/resume. Acknowledge completed purchases within three days; the clock starts after pending becomes purchased. [Integration guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate) | A successful advance purchase should durably issue its future-event reservation promptly. Gate show entry separately. Do not postpone fulfillment/acknowledgement until Sunday. A “pending” or cancelled purchase cannot yield admission. |
| RevenueCat identity | Custom App User IDs are project-scoped and case-sensitive; using the same ID carries purchases across devices. [Identity guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/customers/identifying-customers) | Test account switch during SDK identification, purchase, backend readback, and notification. Preserve durable original ownership while suppressing stale UI results. |
| RevenueCat restore | Default identified-user behavior transfers purchases to the restoring user; configuration applies to new purchases too. Anonymous IDs have aliasing behavior. [Restore behavior](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/projects/restore-behavior) | Actual project setting remains unknown. Test transfer reconciliation and revoked source access; do not infer original ownership solely from the device's store account. Do not change settings during this audit. |
| RevenueCat webhook reliability | Webhooks can duplicate and are retried; event IDs support deduplication. RevenueCat recommends current subscriber readback for synchronization. Sandbox and production transactions can coexist for one customer. [Webhooks](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks) | Persist authenticated processing/dedupe atomically. A crash after ledger insertion but before response must not credit twice. Enforce environment boundaries. |
| RevenueCat event meaning | Cancellation may be unsubscribe or refund; billing issue is not necessarily expiry. Transfer notification goes to the destination. Temporary outage grants can last up to 24 hours. [Event definitions](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks/event-types-and-fields) | Do not equate cancellation with immediate loss of an otherwise paid subscription. Reconcile current validity and both transfer sides. Owner's unknown-billing-state fail-closed rule needs explicit handling of temporary unverified grants. |
| Stripe webhook authentication/order | Verify the raw body signature; deliveries can repeat or arrive out of order. [Webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks) | Event arrival order or timestamp alone cannot authorize grants or resurrect revoked ones. Use stable provider IDs and authoritative state. |
| Stripe idempotency | V1 retries with the same key return the saved result, including server errors; keys may be pruned after at least 24 hours. [Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) | Provider idempotency does not replace durable database uniqueness, transactional capacity allocation, or reconciliation after an uncertain outcome. |
| Stripe payment completion | Checkout session completion and paid status differ; delayed methods finish later. [Stripe engineering explanation](https://stripe.dev/blog/observing-immediate-versus-delayed-payments-with-stripe-workbench), [event definitions](https://docs.stripe.com/api/events/types) | Do not fulfill from redirect/checkout dismissal or `complete` alone. Cover asynchronous success and failure. |
| Refunds / disputes / payouts | Refund status can be pending or failed; refunds use available balances and original payment methods. Connect charge type determines who is debited; recovering transferred money requires reversal handling. [Refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds), [Connect charges](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges) | Distinguish request submitted, provider succeeded, entitlement revoked, ledger adjusted, creator funds recovered, and payout completed. Do not promise immediate refunds or treat estimated creator earnings as available payout funds. |

## Scheduled-show design recommendation (not implemented)

Use a fixed-price virtual admission product by default. Persist the event's UTC start instant plus IANA timezone; display viewer-local time and the event timezone. Capacity means admitted virtual tickets, not numbered physical seats. Offer state, provider transaction, ticket state, room access, replay scope, and payout state should remain distinct.

Suggested production-path cases: last ticket purchased concurrently; duplicate callback; payment completion after a reservation timeout; event cancelled during checkout; session changes during purchase; transaction verified after client network loss; refunded ticket during reconnect; creator delay crossing the sale cutoff; DST gap/fold scheduling; two devices claiming one transaction. These are recommended tests, not claims that the current code passes them.

Owner decisions remain necessary for the sale cutoff, reservation duration and late-payment remedy, multi-ticket/gifting rules, delay/reschedule/no-show handling, cancellation/refund policy, reminder channels, replay inclusion/expiry, and physical seating. Recommend transparent status and support paths while those decisions are pending; do not invent automatic refund or payout guarantees.

## Auctions: separate design decision

Recommendation: fixed-price capacity-limited virtual tickets first; keep VIP separate and leave auctions unimplemented until an explicit design is approved. A potential later auction should charge only a winning buyer for a defined ticket after successful provider verification; bidding itself should not grant access. Reserve rules, winner payment deadline, tie handling, winner failure, capacity allocation, refunds, and anti-abuse controls are additional business behavior.

Compatibility evidence: Apple product prices must be configured before review and use supported price points. [App Store pricing](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase). The current source `_lib/iosAppStoreCommerce.ts` defines four finite tiers (99, 299, 499, 999 minor USD reference units) for event/seat/VIP/video/tip consumables, and exact amount-to-tier resolution; `_lib/revenuecat.ts` purchases a resolved store product. Therefore an arbitrary winning price is **not supported by the current catalog**. This is an inference from source and documentation, not a categorical claim that all digital auctions are prohibited. Do not silently round bids, change SKUs/prices, introduce a wallet, or redirect mobile checkout.

Stripe explicitly lists bidding-fee auctions as prohibited. [US restricted-business list](https://stripe.com/us/legal/restricted-businesses) (updated 2026-09-22). That does not prove provider approval of a conventional highest-bid auction. Any proposed model needs platform/provider eligibility assessment and owner approval before implementation; no provider approval was requested here.
